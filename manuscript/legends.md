Figure 1. Study overview and principal component analysis of RNA fractions. (A) The workflow summarizes the study design and analysis of RNA abundance and redistribution with Monopoly. (B–D) Principal component analyses show gene-level RNA profiles for Input, Monosome, and Polysome libraries, respectively. (E) The combined analysis includes all three RNA fractions. Each point represents one library. Colors indicate ASD (red) or Unaffected (green), while circles, triangles, and squares indicate Input, Monosome, and Polysome, respectively.

Figure 2. P/M analysis reveals RNA redistribution beyond changes in overall abundance. (A) The volcano plot shows differences in Input RNA abundance between ASD and Unaffected. (B) The six most significant GO overrepresentation results include neuronal development terms among genes with lower Input abundance in ASD. Dot size indicates the number of overlapping genes, and bold labels identify neural terms. (C) Input and P/M effects are compared for the ten protein-coding genes with the largest significant P/M changes in either direction. Circles show Input effects, with filled circles indicating significance; diamonds show P/M effects. Bars show 95% confidence intervals. (D) The volcano plot shows differences in P/M ratios. Orange and blue indicate significant increases and decreases in ASD, respectively. (E–F) Mean log₂(TPM + 1) values across three cultures per background are shown for PHOX2A and TOP2A. Their fitted P/M ratios are 2.9-fold higher and 4.8-fold lower in ASD, respectively. Red indicates ASD and green indicates Unaffected. (G) Both ASD-associated gene sets show enrichment toward higher Monosome abundance and lower P/M. (H) Omitting ASD1 makes both P/M enrichment results nonsignificant. (I) ASD-associated and synaptic translation gene sets favor lower P/M, while synaptic vesicle membrane gene sets favor higher P/M. Colors in G–I indicate normalized enrichment scores (NES), with slate indicating negative and magenta indicating positive enrichment. Stars in G and filled markers in H indicate BH-adjusted P < 0.05. Larger dots in I indicate smaller FDR q-values.

Figure 3. Transcript-level analysis reveals additional P/M signals and associations with mRNA structure. (A) Gene-level and transcript-derived P/M evidence are compared for genes tested by both approaches. Transcript analysis identifies 48 additional genes, while five are significant in both analyses and nine only at the gene level. Dashed lines mark BH-adjusted P = 0.05. (B) Transcript structures, Input RNA shares, and P/M changes are shown for the five tested DLK1 transcripts. DLK1-201 has a fitted P/M ratio approximately 2.9-fold higher in ASD. Open and dark blocks indicate untranslated regions (UTRs) and coding sequence (CDS), respectively. (C) Within-gene comparisons associate longer coding sequences with relatively higher P/M in ASD and longer 3′ UTRs with lower P/M. Standardized coefficients summarize associations with P/M test statistics, after accounting for transcript abundance and its share of the gene’s RNA. Candidate upstream open reading frames are labeled uORFs. (D) Among transcripts with identical 5′ UTR and coding sequences, longer 3′ UTRs remain associated with relatively lower P/M in ASD. Points show adjusted transcript effects, and the line summarizes the association. Orange and blue in B–C indicate higher and lower P/M associations, respectively; filled markers indicate BH-adjusted P < 0.05. Bars in B–C and shading in D show 95% confidence intervals. The P value in D is unadjusted.

Table 1. Monopoly combines high detection power with near-nominal FDR under equal dispersion. Results are averaged across 200 simulated datasets per dispersion setting, each with 20 participants per condition and 5,000 genes. In each dataset, 500 genes had a twofold increase or decrease in P/M ratio, and another 500 changed in overall RNA abundance. Power is the percentage of genes with altered P/M ratios detected at BH-adjusted P ≤ 0.05. FDR is the mean percentage of discoveries that were false. Effect RMSE summarizes error in estimated log₂ P/M changes for genes with true mean Input counts ≥20. Monopoly achieved higher power than both comparators and ran faster than edgeR. Under unequal dispersion, its power exceeded edgeR’s (48.1% versus 39.8%), accompanied by a higher FDR (18.3% versus 11.8%).
